# Methodology

## Problem

The system must choose one of eight actions from logged interactions generated by a
historical policy. Only the outcome of the chosen action is observed. There is no
simulator and no counterfactual label for the alternatives.

This creates two separate problems:

1. learn a useful action ranking without treating selection-biased rewards as causal;
2. decide whether the learned policy is credible where it departs from historical behavior.

## Policy architecture

The policy has three layers:

1. A value model estimates immediate reward for every state-action pair.
2. A behavior model estimates which actions have historical support in the current state.
3. Explicit constraints remove structurally invalid or high-risk actions before selection.

The learned model ranks actions only inside the admissible set. Safety and product authority
remain readable code rather than latent model behavior.

## Evaluation

The demonstration reports four complementary estimators:

- inverse propensity scoring (IPS);
- self-normalized IPS (SNIPS);
- a doubly robust estimate combining direct modeling with a propensity-weighted residual;
- fitted-Q evaluation for multi-step trajectory value.

Effective sample size is reported beside value. A large estimate with little overlap is a
warning about extrapolation, not a stronger result. Uncertainty should be resampled at the
episode level because decisions within a trajectory are correlated.

## Leakage controls

Only decision-time state fields enter the policy. Reward, termination, next state, and logged
propensity remain evaluation metadata. Training and evaluation split on complete episode IDs,
never individual rows.

## Limits

The included generator is a deterministic synthetic environment designed to make the pipeline
runnable without restricted data. Its numbers do not estimate deployment impact. Offline policy
evaluation cannot establish true online lift when the historical policy provides little support;
the safe next step would be shadow evaluation followed by a guarded experiment with predefined
rollback criteria.
